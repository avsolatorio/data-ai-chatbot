### Data Gaps & Limitations (for your briefing)

You can explicitly flag these caveats:

1. **Coverage and timeliness**
   - Most series run to **2024**, but **tariffs only to 2022** (WTO/WITS processing lag).

2. **Conceptual issues**
   - **Exports/imports and GDP** are based on **national accounts (SNA 2008)** and **BoP**. They may:
     - miss informal cross‑border trade and smuggling;  
     - be affected by **valuation and timing differences** between customs and BoP.
   - **Merchandise trade (% of GDP)**:
     - excludes some categories (e.g., non‑monetary gold, merchanting), so it is not literally “all cross‑border goods flows.”

3. **Tariffs as an incomplete measure of openness**
   - Indicator is a **simple (unweighted) mean** of applied rates:
     - every tariff line counts equally, regardless of actual trade volume.
     - It ignores **nontariff barriers** (quotas, licensing, SPS measures, etc.), which are often important in South Asia.
   - Thus, a high or low simple mean tariff is indicative but not a full picture of trade restrictiveness.

4. **FDI data limitations**
   - FDI (% of GDP) excludes:
     - capital raised locally by foreign‑owned firms,  
     - intra‑firm trade in services and IP,  
     - some special‑purpose entities and round‑tripping.  
   - Country practices differ on reinvested earnings and intra‑company loans, so cross‑country comparisons should be treated as **approximate**.

5. **Comparability across countries**
   - All four are using standard international manuals (SNA/BPM), but:
     - differences in statistical capacity, base years, and coverage can affect levels.
