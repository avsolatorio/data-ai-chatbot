  - Strengthen **capacity of district and village governments** to plan, budget, and implement pro‑poor investments (building on the Village Law).
  - Use **performance‑based grants** to reward local governments that close service gaps and improve human capital.
- **Territorial development strategies**
  - Adopt **spatially explicit plans** that coordinate infrastructure, land use, and economic development in lagging regions.
  - Integrate **disaster and climate risks** into local planning, especially in flood‑ and landslide‑prone rural areas.

### 5. Human capital in rural and lagging regions
- **Education**
  - Reduce **rural–urban gaps in learning** through teacher deployment, school quality improvements, and targeted support to poor students.
- **Health and nutrition**
  - Focus on **stunting reduction** and maternal/child health in high‑poverty rural districts.
  - Strengthen **primary health care networks** in remote areas.

## Philippines: Key policy directions on rural poverty and spatial inequality

### 1. Raise agricultural and rural productivity
- **Transform smallholder agriculture**
  - Improve **land tenure security** and land administration to encourage investment.
  - Expand **irrigation, post‑harvest facilities, storage, and rural logistics**.
  - Promote **climate‑smart agriculture** to manage typhoon, flood, and drought risks.
- **Diversify rural livelihoods**
  - Support **off‑farm and non‑farm rural jobs** (services, agro‑processing, tourism).
  - Expand **credit and extension services** tailored to small farmers and fishers.

### 2. Prioritize lagging and conflict‑affected regions (especially Mindanao)
- **Targeted regional development**
  - Focus public investment in **Mindanao and other high‑poverty, conflict‑affected areas** (roads, ports, power, digital).
  - Strengthen **peace and development programs** in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region and surrounding provinces.
- **Service delivery in remote and fragile areas**
  - Improve **health, education, and social services** in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDAs).
  - Use **mobile and community‑based service delivery** where fixed facilities are hard to sustain.

### 3. Manage urbanization to reduce spatial inequality
- **Balanced urban–rural development**
  - Invest in **secondary cities and growth corridors** to spread jobs and services beyond Metro Manila.
  - Improve **inter‑island and inter‑regional connectivity** (ports, airports, roads, ferries) to integrate rural producers with urban markets.
- **Urban planning and housing**
  - Strengthen **urban planning, land use regulation, and affordable housing** to prevent the growth of high‑risk informal settlements that trap migrants from rural areas.

### 4. Strengthen social protection and disaster risk management
- **Shock‑responsive safety nets**
  - Expand and modernize programs like **conditional cash transfers** and other social assistance to better cover:
    - rural poor,  
    - farmers and fishers affected by climate and price shocks.
  - Make programs more **adaptive to disasters** (automatic triggers, scalable benefits).
- **Disaster risk reduction with spatial focus**
  - Prioritize **risk‑informed infrastructure and land‑use planning** in high‑hazard rural and coastal areas.
  - Support **community‑based disaster preparedness** and early warning systems.

### 5. Improve local governance and decentralization outcomes
- **Capacity and incentives for LGUs**
  - Strengthen **local government capacity** (planning, PFM, procurement) in poorer provinces and municipalities.
  - Use **performance‑based grants and transparent metrics** to reward LGUs that reduce service gaps and improve poverty outcomes.
- **Data and targeting**
  - Improve **local poverty, service, and hazard data** to better target programs to poor rural barangays and municipalities.

## Common cross‑country themes in World Bank advice

Across both Indonesia and the Philippines, World Bank recommendations converge on:

1. **Investing in lagging regions** (infrastructure, services, and connectivity) to reduce structural spatial disadvantages.  
2. **Raising rural productivity** (agriculture and non‑farm) to make staying in or near rural areas a viable path out of poverty.  
3. **Strengthening social protection** so that rural and remote households are protected from shocks and can invest in human capital.  
4. **Improving local governance and spatial planning**, ensuring that decentralization actually reduces, rather than reinforces, regional inequality.  
5. **Integrating climate and disaster risk management** into rural and regional development strategies.
