## 1. Where to find the reports

### (a) Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS) – Nigeria

1. Go to **The DHS Program** website: https://dhsprogram.com
2. Top menu → **“Publications” → “Country Reports”**.
3. Filter:

   - Earlier DHS (e.g., 2013) if you need a longer trend.

These are downloadable as **PDFs** (and often also as HTML).

### (b) National Nutrition and Health Survey (NNHS), SMART‑type surveys

NNHS reports are not on DHS; they are usually hosted by Nigerian authorities and partners:

- Federal Ministry of Health / National Bureau of Statistics (NBS)
- National Primary Health Care Development Agency (NPHCDA)
- UNICEF Nigeria / Nutrition section
- Sometimes on **ReliefWeb** or **UNICEF** global document repository.

Practical search approach:

- In a browser, search for:  
  - `“National Nutrition and Health Survey Nigeria 2018 pdf”`  
  - `“NNHS Nigeria 2021 SMART survey pdf”`  
- Look for results from domains like:
  - `nigerianstat.gov.ng` (NBS)
  - `health.gov.ng`
  - `unicef.org` / `unicef.org/nigeria`
  - `reliefweb.int`

You are aiming for reports titled along the lines of:  
- “National Nutrition and Health Survey (NNHS) 2018 – Report”  
- “… 2021 NNHS” etc.

<hr>

## 2. How to navigate the DHS report for zone‑level nutrition

Using the **Nigeria DHS 2018** as an example (structure is very standard):

### Step 1 – Find the nutrition chapter

1. Open the PDF.
2. Use the **Table of Contents** or search (Ctrl+F) for:
   - “**Nutritional Status of Children**”
   - Often Chapter 11 or similar (exact number varies).

This chapter contains tables for **stunting, wasting, underweight, overweight**.

### Step 2 – Identify the right tables

Within the nutrition chapter, look for tables with titles like:

- “**Nutritional status of children by background characteristics**”
- “**Nutritional status of children under five years by background characteristics**”

Typical DHS table codes are something like **Table 11.2, 11.3, etc.**

These tables usually have:

- **Rows** = background characteristics (including **region**, **mother’s education**, **wealth quintile**, etc.)
- **Columns** = nutrition indicators:
  - Height‑for‑age (stunted: <‑2 SD; severely stunted: <‑3 SD)
  - Weight‑for‑height (wasted; severely wasted)
  - Weight‑for‑age (underweight; severely underweight)
  - Sometimes overweight.

### Step 3 – Extract North East and North West values

In the “background characteristics” rows, you will see:

- **Region / Geopolitical zone** rows:
  - North Central
  - **North East**
  - **North West**
  - South East
  - South South
  - South West

For each of **North East** and **North West**, read across the row to get:

- % **stunted** (<‑2 SD) and % **severely stunted** (<‑3 SD)
- % **wasted** and **severely wasted**
- % **underweight** and **severely underweight**
- The **number of children (N)** in the denominator – important for interpreting reliability.

When you write your report, you can say, for example (paraphrasing the structure, not numbers):

> “According to the 2018 Nigeria DHS, the prevalence of stunting among children under 5 was X% in the North East and Y% in the North West, compared with a national average of Z%. The proportion of children who were wasted was … etc.”

### Step 4 – Pay attention to sampling and significance

DHS tables include:

- **N** (number of children measured) for each subgroup.
- Sometimes **sampling errors / confidence intervals** in an annex.

When interpreting:

- Be cautious with **small Ns** (e.g., if a subgroup has a small sample, the estimate is less precise).
- For major zones like North East / North West, Ns are usually large enough for robust estimates.

## 3. How to navigate NNHS (SMART) reports for zone‑level nutrition

NNHS/SMART reports are designed **specifically** for nutrition and often have **more recent** and **more frequent** data than DHS.

### Step 1 – Find the anthropometry section

Once you open an NNHS report:

- Use the **Contents** or search for:
  - “**Anthropometric results**”
  - “**Nutritional status of children 6–59 months**”
  - “**Prevalence of stunting/wasting/underweight**”

### Step 2 – Locate tables by geopolitical zone

NNHS typically presents:

- **National prevalence**
- **By geopolitical zone** (North East, North West, etc.)
- Sometimes **by state**.

Look for tables titled like:

- “Prevalence of wasting (weight‑for‑height z‑score) among children 6–59 months, by geopolitical zone”
- “Prevalence of stunting (height‑for‑age z‑score) among children 6–59 months, by state and zone”

Columns usually include:

- % **Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM)** – WHZ <‑2 SD (and/or MUAC)
