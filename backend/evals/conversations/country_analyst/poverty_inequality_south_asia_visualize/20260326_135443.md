## Data

### 1. World Bank – Global poverty at $2.15/day (2017 PPP)

- **Indicator (World Bank Global Subnational / PIP):**  
  - Database: `WB_GS`  
  - Indicator ID: `WB_GS_SI_POV_DDAY`  
  - Name: *Poverty headcount ratio at $2.15 a day (2017 PPP) (% of population)*  
- **What it measures:** Share of people living on less than $2.15/day at 2017 PPP prices (the current **extreme poverty** line).  
- **Comparability:** Designed explicitly for **cross‑country comparison**, including India and Bangladesh, using a common PPP‑based line and harmonized methods.  
- **Coverage:** Annual series, roughly **1960–2023** (actual availability for India/Bangladesh depends on survey years).  
- **Key metadata points (for your methods section):**  
  - International poverty line updated to **$2.15/day in 2017 PPP**; earlier editions used different lines and PPP rounds, so **you should not compare these new series to pre‑2022 published rates** based on older PPPs.  
  - Based on **household consumption or income surveys**; when consumption is not available, income is used.  
  - Limitations: infrequent surveys in some years; differences in survey design and quality; PPP revisions mean older published poverty rates are not directly comparable.

**Use in your report:**  
- For **headline, internationally comparable extreme poverty trends** (India vs Bangladesh, over time).  
- Good for charts like “share of population under $2.15/day, 1990–2023”.

<hr>

### 2. World Bank – National poverty lines

- **Indicator (World Development Indicators):**  
  - Database: `WB_WDI`  
  - Indicator ID: `WB_WDI_SI_POV_NAHC`  
  - Name: *Poverty headcount ratio at national poverty lines (% of population)*  
- **What it measures:** Share of people below each country’s **own national poverty line** (which reflects local standards and baskets).  
- **Comparability:**  
  - **Comparable over time within a country**, but **not suitable for cross‑country level comparisons**, because the poverty lines differ and rise with income levels.  
- **Coverage:** For India and Bangladesh, survey‑based data from the mid‑1980s up to the most recent survey (metadata range: **1985–2024**, but actual years are discrete survey years).  
- **Key metadata points:**  
  - National lines typically anchored in a **nutrition‑based food basket + non‑food allowance**.  
  - Series includes only estimates that are **reasonably comparable over time within a country**.  
  - For methodological transparency, note that India and Bangladesh periodically **re‑base or revise** their national lines.

**Use in your report:**  
- For **within‑country narratives**, e.g. “India’s national poverty rate fell from X% to Y% between year A and B,” and similarly for Bangladesh.  
- Compare **trends** across the two countries (e.g. “both declined, but India’s decline started earlier”), but avoid claiming that “India’s poverty is higher/lower than Bangladesh” based solely on these national‑line series.

<hr>

### 3. World Bank – Inequality: Gini index

- **Indicator (World Development Indicators):**  
  - Database: `WB_WDI`  
  - Indicator ID: `WB_WDI_SI_POV_GINI`  
  - Name: *Gini index*  
- **What it measures:** Standard **Gini coefficient** of income or (preferably) consumption distribution; **0 = perfect equality**, **100 = perfect inequality**.  
- **Comparability:**  
  - Methods harmonized as far as possible, but underlying surveys differ (consumption vs income, questionnaire design, etc.), so **cross‑country comparisons are indicative, not exact**.  
- **Coverage:** Metadata range **1963–2024**; for India/Bangladesh, inequality observations are tied to survey years.  
- **Key metadata points:**  
  - Where possible, the World Bank uses **consumption** (less unequal and a better welfare measure in low‑ and middle‑income countries).  
  - Not additive across groups (you can’t make a regional Gini by averaging national ones).  
  - Same Gini value can correspond to different Lorenz curves; it’s a summary index.

**Use in your report:**  
- To compare **inequality levels and trends**: “India’s Gini has been roughly stable / rising / falling compared to Bangladesh since year X.”  
- Combine with poverty measures to discuss **“pro‑poor” growth** or distributional patterns.

### 4. World Bank – Inequality: income share of the poorest

- **Indicator (World Development Indicators):**  
  - Database: `WB_WDI`  
  - Indicator ID: `WB_WDI_SI_DST_FRST_20`  
  - Name: *Income share held by lowest 20%*  
- **What it measures:** Percentage of **total income or consumption** accruing to the **bottom 20% of the population**.  
- **Comparability:** Same caveats as Gini: harmonized, but survey differences remain.  
- **Coverage:** Metadata range **1963–2024**, with observations for India and Bangladesh in survey years.  
- **Key metadata points:**  
  - Derived from the same household surveys used for Gini.  
  - Shares by quintile may not sum to 100% due to rounding.

**Use in your report:**  
- To give a **distributional angle** that is easier to interpret than the Gini:  
  - “In Bangladesh, the bottom 20% receive X% of total income, versus Y% in India.”  
- Useful for **equity‑of‑growth** discussion: if poverty fell but the bottom 20% share stagnated or declined, growth may have been less inclusive.

### 5. World Bank – Multidimensional poverty (UNDP MPI series)

- **Indicator (World Development Indicators):**  
  - Database: `WB_WDI`  
  - Indicator ID: `WB_WDI_SI_POV_MPUN`
