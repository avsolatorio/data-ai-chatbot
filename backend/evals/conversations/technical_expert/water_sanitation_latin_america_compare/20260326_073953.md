## Data limitations and integration caveats

When integrating these indicators into your analytical pipeline, there are several important caveats:

1. **Model‑based estimates (not pure survey counts)**  
   - Both indicators are produced by the **WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme (JMP)** using **regression models and interpolation/extrapolation** from multiple data sources.  
   - Values like <claim id="0af40f09" policy="auto">100.00</claim>% or <claim id="d621dca1" policy="auto">100.00</claim>% should be treated as **rounded model outputs**, not exact census counts.

2. **Different coverage thresholds**  
   - **Safely managed water (`SMDW`)**: country estimates only when data are available on **water quality** and at least one of **accessibility** or **availability**; aggregates require ≥30% population coverage.  
   - **Basic water (`BASW`)**: national, regional, and income‑group estimates require **≥50% population coverage**.  
   - Comparing these two indicators directly or mixing them in composite indices should account for these different sampling thresholds.

3. **Irregular time series & “wave” updates**  
   - Safely managed water is not updated annually for all countries; years like 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024 are more common.  
   - Linear interpolation between JMP points is already baked into the published series; **avoid re‑interpolating** in your own pipeline.

4. **Conceptual differences: basic vs safely managed**  
   - **Basic** = improved source + collection time ≤30 minutes round trip.  
   - **Safely managed** = improved source + on‑premises + available when needed + free from contamination.  
   - For SDG tracking and health outcomes, safely managed is more relevant; for infrastructure access or affordability analyses, basic may still be useful.

5. **Cross‑country comparability constraints**  
   - Service definitions are standardized, but **data inputs differ** by country (survey types, administrative systems, frequency).  
   - Some countries have more frequent water quality testing; others rely on fewer data points, increasing uncertainty.

6. **Sanitation missing from this slice**  
   - The sanitation indicator I attempted to fetch (`SH_STNT_ZS`) was not available via the specific ID I used. For a full WASH view, you will want:  
     - Basic sanitation;  
     - Safely managed sanitation;  
     - Possibly handwashing facilities (SDG 6.2.1).

## Data limitations and integration notes (sanitation)

These sanitation indicators share many caveats with the water indicators:

1. **Model‑based JMP estimates**  
   - Both `SH_STA_BASS_ZS` and `SH_STA_SMSS_ZS` are produced via **regression models** using surveys, censuses, and administrative data.  
   - Year‑to‑year movements (e.g., from <claim id="7305ec5a" policy="auto">89.56</claim>% to <claim id="31f83b6d" policy="auto">90.32</claim>% in Brazil) may reflect modeling updates, not new surveys.

2. **Different coverage thresholds**  
   - Basic sanitation estimates require ≥50% population coverage in underlying data; safely managed sanitation requires ≥30% coverage and sometimes uses **assumptions** (e.g., 50% safely managed where no treatment data exist).  
   - This makes safely managed values more uncertain and less directly comparable.

3. **Conceptual differences**  
   - **Basic sanitation**: improved, **not shared**, but no requirement on excreta treatment.  
   - **Safely managed sanitation**: improved, **not shared**, and excreta are **safely disposed or treated** (on‑site or off‑site).  
   - For health risk modeling, safely managed is more relevant; for infrastructure access, basic may be sufficient.

4. **Irregular underlying data**  
   - JMP publishes in “waves”; intermediate years can be interpolations. Avoid re‑interpolating or smoothing aggressively in your pipeline.

5. **Consistency with water indicators**  
   - These are conceptually aligned with the water indicators you already use (`BASW` and `SMDW`). For composite WASH metrics, you can:
     - Pair **basic water** with **basic sanitation**;  
     - Pair **safely managed water** with **safely managed sanitation**;  
     - Keep them separate but comparable across countries and over time.
