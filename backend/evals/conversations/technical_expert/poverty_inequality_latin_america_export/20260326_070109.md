## 1. National poverty (Brazil’s own poverty line)

### 1.1 Poverty headcount ratio at national poverty lines (% of population)

- **Database / indicator ID:**  
  - `WB_HNP` — `WB_HNP_SI_POV_NAHC`  
- **Concept:** Share of Brazil’s population living below the **national (country-specific) poverty line**. Best for **within‑Brazil** policy work.

**Key metadata (methodology summary)**  
- Indicator measures the **percentage of the population living below Brazil’s national poverty line(s)**.  
- Based on **nationally representative household surveys**, with income/consumption aggregates constructed from detailed survey data.  
- National lines are typically anchored to a **food bundle plus non‑food allowance**, adjusted for inflation over time.  
- Good for tracking poverty **over time within Brazil**, but **not for cross‑country comparison**, because each country’s line reflects its own standard of minimum needs.

**API endpoint (Brazil, 2019–2024)**

## 2. International poverty lines (2021 PPP) – World Bank PIP & WDI

These are crucial for **cross‑country‑comparable** poverty analysis.

### 2.1 Poverty headcount ratio at $3.00/day (2021 PPP) – PIP

- **Database / indicator ID:**  
  - `WB_PIP` — `WB_PIP_HEADCOUNT_IPL`
- **Concept:** Share of the population living under the **$3.00/day (2021 PPP)** global extreme poverty line.

**Key metadata**  
- Welfare is **consumption or income per person**, in **2021 PPP US dollars**.  
- Data are from **household surveys**, with extrapolations/interpolations using national accounts growth when survey years are missing.  
- Methodology and limitations documented here:  
  - https://datanalytics.worldbank.org/PIP-Methodology/  
- Includes **urban/rural disaggregation** via `URBANISATION` dimension (`URB`, `RUR`, `_T`).

**API endpoint (Brazil, 2019–2024, total population)**

```text
https://data360api.worldbank.org/data360/data?DATABASE_ID=WB_PIP&INDICATOR=WB_PIP_HEADCOUNT_IPL&REF_AREA=BRA&timePeriodFrom=2019&timePeriodTo=2024&URBANISATION=_T&top=1000
```

You can change `URBANISATION=_T` to `URB` or `RUR` to split Brazil’s poverty by urban/rural.

### 2.2 Poverty headcount ratio at $8.30/day (2021 PPP) – upper‑middle‑income line

- **Database / indicator ID:**  
  - `WB_WDI` — `WB_WDI_SI_POV_UMIC`
- **Concept:** Share of population living on **less than $8.30/day (2021 PPP)**, a line typical of **upper‑middle‑income** countries (relevant for Brazil).

**Key metadata**  
- Uses **international poverty lines** based on 2021 PPPs.  
- $8.30/day line derived from typical national poverty lines in **upper‑middle‑income** economies.  
- Based on **household consumption or income surveys**; consumption is preferred where available.  
- Good for assessing **vulnerability / near‑poor** above the extreme $3.00 line.

**API endpoint (Brazil, 2019–2024)**

## 3. Inequality – Gini index

### 3.1 Gini index (WDI – standard series)

- **Database / indicator ID:**  
  - `WB_WDI` — `WB_WDI_SI_POV_GINI`
- **Concept:** **Gini coefficient** of income or consumption distribution (0 = perfect equality, 100 = maximum inequality).

**Key metadata**  
- Based on **nationally representative household surveys** (income or consumption; consumption preferred).  
- Computed from **Lorenz curve** of the distribution; area between Lorenz and line of equality, as % of maximum area.  
- Not directly additive across groups; cannot aggregate country Ginis into a regional Gini.  
- Comparability issues across countries/years due to differences in survey methods, welfare concept (income vs consumption), and household definitions.

**API endpoint (Brazil, 2019–2024)**

```text
https://data360api.worldbank.org/data360/data?DATABASE_ID=WB_WDI&INDICATOR=WB_WDI_SI_POV_GINI&REF_AREA=BRA&timePeriodFrom=2019&timePeriodTo=2024&top=1000
```

This is a closely related series, often used in World Bank monitoring platforms.

- **Database / indicator ID:**  
  - `WB_GS` — `WB_GS_SI_POV_GINI`
- **Concept & methodology:** Same Gini definition; series is curated for **global monitoring**, with harmonization efforts across surveys.

**API endpoint (Brazil, 2019–2024)**

## 3. Inequality – Gini index

### 3.1 Gini index (WDI – standard series)

- **Database / indicator ID:**  
  - `WB_WDI` — `WB_WDI_SI_POV_GINI`
- **Concept:** **Gini coefficient** of income or consumption distribution (0 = perfect equality, 100 = maximum inequality).

**Key metadata**  
- Based on **nationally representative household surveys** (income or consumption; consumption preferred).  
- Computed from **Lorenz curve** of the distribution; area between Lorenz and line of equality, as % of maximum area.  
- Not directly additive across groups; cannot aggregate country Ginis into a regional Gini.  
- Comparability issues across countries/years due to differences in survey methods, welfare concept (income vs consumption), and household definitions.

**API endpoint (Brazil, 2019–2024)**

```text
https://data360api.worldbank.org/data360/data?DATABASE_ID=WB_WDI&INDICATOR=WB_WDI_SI_POV_GINI&REF_AREA=BRA&timePeriodFrom=2019&timePeriodTo=2024&top=1000
```

## 5. How to integrate programmatically (Python template)

import requests
import pandas as pd

url = (
    "https://data360api.worldbank.org/data360/data"
    "?DATABASE_ID=WB_WDI"
    "&INDICATOR=WB_WDI_SI_POV_GINI"
    "&REF_AREA=BRA"

    "&timePeriodTo=2024"

j = r.json()

# Data are usually under j["data"]
df = pd.DataFrame(j["data"])

# - INDICATOR
# - OBS_VALUE (the numeric value)

```
