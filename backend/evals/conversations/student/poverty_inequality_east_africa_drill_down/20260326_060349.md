<hr>

### 1. Monetary poverty – “who is poor?”

1. **National poverty headcount ratio**  
   - Example indicator: *“Poverty headcount ratio at national poverty lines (% of population)”* (WB_WDI_SI_POV_NAHC).  
   - **What it measures:** Share of Kenyans whose consumption/income is below Kenya’s own official poverty line (set in Kenyan shillings, based on local prices and a basic needs basket).  
   - **Why it matters:**  
     - Used by the Government of Kenya for national poverty targets.  
     - Captures local cost of living better than a single global line.  
   - **Typical use in assignments:** “X% of Kenyans lived below the national poverty line in year Y; this is down/up from year Z.”

2. **International poverty headcount ratios (global poverty lines)**  
   Main World Bank lines (in 2021 PPP terms) that show up for Kenya:
   - *$3.00 a day* (lower-middle-income poverty line)  
     - Indicator examples:  
       - “Poverty headcount ratio (2021 PPP, $3.00) (% of population)” (WB_PIP_HEADCOUNT_IPL)  
       - “Poverty gap at $3.00 a day (2021 PPP) (%)” (WB_WDI_SI_POV_GAPS).  
   - *$4.20 a day* (upper-middle-income line for LMICs/UMICs)  
     - Indicators: headcount ratio and poverty gap (WB_WDI_SI_POV_LMIC, WB_WDI_SI_POV_LMIC_GP).  
   - *$8.30 a day* (higher vulnerability line)  
     - Indicator: “Poverty headcount ratio at $8.30 a day (2021 PPP) (% of population)” (WB_WDI_SI_POV_UMIC).  

   - **What they measure:** Percentage of people living below each global line, after adjusting for price differences across countries (PPP).  
   - **Why they matter:**  
     - Allow **cross‑country comparisons** (Kenya vs. other countries).  
     - Show different **depths of poverty/vulnerability**:  
       - $3.00: more extreme poverty in a lower‑middle‑income context.  
       - $4.20 and $8.30: people who are not extremely poor but still vulnerable.  

3. **Number of poor (headcount in millions)**  
   - Example: *“Population living below the poverty line (2021 PPP, $3.00) (million)”* (WB_PIP_NPOOR_IPL).  
   - **What it measures:** The **absolute number** of Kenyans below the poverty line, in millions.  
   - **Why it matters:**  
     - Converts percentages into people – useful for discussing scale of need and for targeting programs (“about X million people”).  

<hr>

### 2. Poverty depth and severity – “how poor are the poor?”

1. **Poverty gap index**  
   - Examples for Kenya:  
     - “Poverty gap at $3.00 a day (2021 PPP) (%)” (WB_WDI_SI_POV_GAPS).  
     - “Poverty gap at $4.20 a day (2021 PPP) (%)” (WB_WDI_SI_POV_LMIC_GP).  
   - **What it measures:**  
     - The **average shortfall** from the poverty line, as a percentage of that line, across the whole population.  
     - Equals 0 if no one is poor; increases when poor people are **further below** the line.  
   - **Interpretation example:**  
     - A poverty gap of 5% at $3.00 means that, on average, the poor are 5% below that line, and if you could perfectly target transfers, spending 5% of the poverty line per person (on average) would close the gap.  
   - **Why it matters:**  
     - Two countries can have the same headcount but very different gaps; the gap shows **depth**, not just incidence.

2. **(Less often in quick summaries but conceptually important):**  
   - **Squared poverty gap / severity (Foster–Greer–Thorbecke α=2)** – not in your search list by name, but often discussed in theory. It gives more weight to the **poorest among the poor**.

<hr>

### 3. Multidimensional and “societal” poverty – beyond income

1. **Multidimensional poverty headcount ratio**  
   - Example: *“Multidimensional poverty headcount ratio (World Bank) (% of population)”* (WB_WDI_SI_POV_MPWB).  
   - **What it measures:**  
     - Share of people who are deprived in multiple dimensions at once (e.g., education, health, housing, basic services), not just income.  
   - **Why it matters for Kenya:**  
     - In Kenya, people may have incomes above a poverty line but still lack **electricity, safe water, schooling, or decent housing**.  
     - Useful when discussing **SDGs** and “leaving no one behind”.
