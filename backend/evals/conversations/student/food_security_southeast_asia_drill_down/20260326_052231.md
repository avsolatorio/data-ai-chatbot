## 1. Chronic hunger / undernourishment

### a) Prevalence of undernourishment (FAO and WDI/SDG versions)

**Main series:**

1. **FAO – Prevalence of undernourishment (percent) (3‑year average)**  
   - ID: **FAO_FS_210041** (database: *Suite of Food Security Indicators*)  
   - Concept: Probability that a randomly selected person consumes **too few calories** to meet the minimum energy requirement for an active, healthy life.  
   - Key points:
     - Based on a **probability distribution** of habitual dietary energy intake vs. a **minimum dietary energy requirement** threshold.
     - Uses **3‑year averages** (e.g., 2022 refers to the 2020–2022 average) to smooth out short‑term fluctuations.
     - Measures **chronic** undernourishment, not short‑term shocks.

2. **World Bank WDI – Prevalence of undernourishment (% of population)**  
   - ID: **WB_WDI_SN_ITK_DEFC_ZS** (World Development Indicators)  
   - SDG link: This is the World Bank’s version of **SDG 2.1.1**.  
   - Definition is essentially the same as FAO’s:  
     > Percentage of the population whose habitual food consumption is insufficient to provide the dietary energy levels required for a normal active and healthy life.  
   - Data source: FAO; WDI mainly repackages it with World Bank country/region aggregates.

3. **UN SDG – 2.1.1 Prevalence of undernourishment**  
   - ID: **UN_SDG_SN_ITK_DEFC** (UN SDG Database)  
   - Concept, method, and limitations are the same as the WDI series; this is the official **SDG 2.1.1** reporting channel.

4. **FAO – Number of people undernourished (million) (3‑year average)**  
   - ID: **FAO_FS_210011**  
   - Concept: **Headcount** (number of people) undernourished, derived by applying the prevalence of undernourishment to total population.  
   - Good for translating percentages into **absolute numbers of people** affected.

**How to use these in a Philippines project:**

- For **headline chronic hunger trends** in the Philippines:
  - Use either FAO_FS_210041 or WB_WDI_SN_ITK_DEFC_ZS (they share the same FAO source).
  - If you need **SDG framing**, mention SDG 2.1.1 and you can reference the UN_SDG series.
- If you need to say “how many Filipinos are undernourished,” combine:
  - Prevalence (%) with  
  - Number of people undernourished (FAO_FS_210011) for intuitive communication.

## 2. Food access: Moderate and severe food insecurity (FIES-based)

These focus on **access to food** (economic and physical), not just calories.

1. **Prevalence of severe food insecurity in the total population (percent) (3‑year average)**  
   - ID: **FAO_FS_210401**  
   - SDG link: One component of **SDG 2.1.2** (food insecurity).  
   - Concept:
     - Percentage of people living in households classified as **severely food insecure**, based on the **Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES)** or compatible tools.
     - Severe food insecurity corresponds to experiences like:
       - Having to **skip meals**,
       - Going **hungry**,
       - Going a **whole day without eating** because of lack of money or other resources.
   - Method:
     - Uses a **Rasch model** (Item Response Theory) to estimate a latent food insecurity scale.
     - Thresholds are calibrated to a **global FIES reference scale** so they’re comparable across countries.
     - Published as 3‑year averages; includes **90% confidence intervals** (upper and lower bounds).

2. **Prevalence of moderate or severe food insecurity in the total population (percent) (3‑year average)**  
   - ID: **FAO_FS_210091**  
   - Concept:
     - Percentage of people in households that are **moderately or severely food insecure**.
     - Moderate food insecurity includes:
       - Worrying about not having enough food,
       - Compromising on **diet quality**,
       - Starting to reduce **food quantity** due to lack of resources.
   - Also an SDG 2.1.2 indicator; same FIES‑based methodology as above.

**Why these matter for the Philippines:**

- They capture **economic access** to food, not just calorie adequacy.
- They are more sensitive to shocks like **price spikes, income loss, or disasters** than the chronic undernourishment measure.
- For an analysis of **inequality or gender**, note that both indicators can be broken down by **sex (Total, Male, Female)** though not all countries/years have sex‑disaggregated data.

## 3. Diet quality: Protein supply

1. **Average protein supply (g/cap/day) (3‑year average)**  
   - ID: **FAO_FS_21013**  
   - Concept:
     - National **average protein available** for human consumption, expressed as **grams per person per day**.
     - Derived from **food balance sheets** (production + imports – exports – losses, etc.), not from household surveys.
   - Use:
     - Proxy for **diet quality** and **nutritional adequacy** (beyond calories).
     - Can be compared to recommended protein intake levels, but remember it’s **availability**, not actual intake.

**How to use for the Philippines:**

- Combine with undernourishment and food insecurity indicators to show that:
  - Even if **average protein supply** is adequate nationally,  
  - **Access and inequality** (captured by FIES‑based indicators) may still leave many people food insecure.
