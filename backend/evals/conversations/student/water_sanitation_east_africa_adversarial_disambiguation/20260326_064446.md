**🧠 Thinking** (stage: interpreting)

You’re running into a very common problem: **many water and sanitation sources use overlapping or confusing place names for Ethiopia.** Let’s untangle the main ones and how they show up in data, so your project is consistent.

<hr>

## 1. Country vs. regions vs. cities

### 1.1. Country name and code

In World Bank / UN-style data:

- Country name: **Ethiopia**
- ISO 3‑letter code: **ETH**

If you’re pulling any World Bank WASH indicators (like access to drinking water or sanitation), always filter by **`REF_AREA = ETH`** (or “Ethiopia”) for national-level data.

<hr>

### 1.2. Administrative regions with similar or changing names

Ethiopia has federal regions (“kililoch”), chartered cities, and zones/woredas. Names can look similar or change over time.

Common sources of confusion:

1. **Addis Ababa vs. Oromia vs. “Finfinne”**
   - **Addis Ababa** – capital city, its own administrative unit.
   - **Oromia Region** – surrounds Addis Ababa.
   - **Finfinne** – Oromo name for Addis Ababa. In some documents (especially local/activist or regional sources) you may see “Finfinne” instead of “Addis Ababa.”
   - In most international datasets: look for **“Addis Ababa”**, not “Finfinne.”

2. **Dire Dawa**
   - A **chartered city** (like Addis Ababa), not part of Oromia or Somali regions, though it’s geographically between them.
   - Sometimes grouped with “urban Ethiopia” in surveys.

3. **Harari / Harar**
   - **Harari Region** – small federal region.
   - **Harar** – city within Harari Region.
   - Some reports use “Harar” when they really mean the region; check context.

4. **Southern regions that have split**
   - Older documents: **“Southern Nations, Nationalities and Peoples’ Region (SNNPR)”**.
   - Recently, parts of SNNPR have become new regions: e.g., **Sidama**, **South West Ethiopia Peoples’ Region**, etc.
   - For time series: early years may have only “SNNPR”; later years show new region names. Be careful when comparing over time.

5. **Somali vs. “Ogaden”**
   - Official region name: **Somali Region**.
   - Older or political texts may say “Ogaden”; use **“Somali”** in data contexts.

<hr>

## 2. Typical geographic breakdowns in water & sanitation data

When you look at WASH data for Ethiopia (e.g., JMP, DHS, World Bank):

You’ll commonly see:

- **National total** – “Ethiopia” or “ETH”
- **Urban / Rural** – “Urban Ethiopia”, “Rural Ethiopia”
- **By region** – Amhara, Oromia, Tigray, Somali, Afar, Benishangul-Gumuz, Gambela, Harari, Sidama, etc.
- **By city** – Addis Ababa, Dire Dawa, sometimes Mekelle, Bahir Dar, etc.

If you see two similar names in the same table, check:

1. Is one a **region** and one a **city**? (e.g., Harari vs. Harar city)
2. Is one an **old name** and one a **new region** (e.g., SNNPR vs. Sidama)?
3. Is one **urban/rural subset** of the other? (e.g., “Urban Oromia” vs. “Oromia total”)

<hr>

## 3. How to keep your project consistent

### 3.1. Pick a naming convention and stick to it

For your report or slides, decide:

- Use **“Addis Ababa”** (with a note that it’s also known as Finfinne).
- Use **current official region names**, but note historical changes:  
  e.g., “SNNPR (before the creation of Sidama and South West Ethiopia Peoples’ Region).”
- Distinguish clearly between:
  - Country (Ethiopia)
  - Region (Oromia)
  - City (Addis Ababa, Dire Dawa)
  - Urban/rural (Urban Ethiopia, Rural Ethiopia)

### 3.2. When reading a paper or dataset

If you’re unsure what a place label means, check:

- **Level**: Is it listed alongside regions, cities, or “urban/rural”? That tells you what scale it is.
- **Year**: If it’s pre–2020, “SNNPR” probably includes areas that are now separate regions.
- **Source note**: Many surveys (DHS, MICS, JMP country files) have a section “Administrative structure” or “Geographic coverage” explaining the boundaries used.

<hr>

## 4. How this connects to your WASH questions

Most international WASH indicators on Ethiopia (like “people using safely managed drinking water services” or “safely managed sanitation services”) are:

- **National-level** (ETH)  
- Sometimes broken down by **urban/rural** or **region** in detailed survey microdata, but not always in headline World Bank tables.

So for a typical project:

- Use **national ETH** data for your main trend graphs.
- Use **urban vs. rural Ethiopia** to show inequalities.
- Where you want regional examples (e.g., Oromia vs. Amhara vs. Somali), make sure you:
  - Use the same regional structure for all years you compare, or
  - Clearly state that boundaries changed and that comparisons are approximate.

<hr>

## 5. Quick checklist you can apply to any confusing name

When you see a place name in a water/sanitation report on Ethiopia, ask:

1. **Is it the country, a region, a city, or an urban/rural category?**
2. **Is it an official name, a local name (e.g., Finfinne), or an older name (e.g., Ogaden, SNNPR)?**
3. **Which year(s) does it apply to, and were boundaries the same then as now?**

If you answer those three, you can almost always “translate” the name to a clear, consistent label for your project.
