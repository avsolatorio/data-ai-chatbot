Here are the main **reliable data sources** you can use for a project on trade openness and economic integration in India, with notes on what each one is best for and how to use it.

---

## 1. World Bank – World Development Indicators (WDI)

**What it’s good for (macro “openness”):**

- Merchandise trade (% of GDP) – canonical trade openness proxy  
  - Indicator: *Merchandise trade (% of GDP)* (in Data360: `WB_WDI_TG_VAL_TOTL_GD_ZS`)  
- Exports of goods and services (% of GDP)  
- Imports of goods and services (% of GDP)  
- Trade in services (exports/imports, % of GDP, current USD)

**Why it’s useful:**

- Long time series for India (often from the 1960s/1970s onward).
- Comparable across countries, good for cross-country benchmarks of India’s openness.

**How to access:**

- World Bank Data site: https://data.worldbank.org  
  - Search “India merchandise trade (% of GDP)”, “exports of goods and services (% of GDP)”.
- Programmatic:
  - World Bank API (JSON/XML) – the WDI page shows the API link for each indicator.

---

## 2. World Bank – World Integrated Trade Solution (WITS)

**What it’s good for (detailed trade structure & policy):**

- Bilateral trade flows by partner and product (HS/SITC).
- Tariff rates (MFN, effectively applied) at detailed product level.
- Non-tariff measures (for some partners).
- Growth in trade values:
  - Example from the search: *Trade Value – Country Growth (%)* (`WB_WITS_CNTRY_GRWTH`) and *Trade Value – World Growth (%)* (`WB_WITS_WRLD_GRWTH`).

**Why it’s useful:**

- Lets you look at India’s integration with specific partners (e.g., EU, US, ASEAN, SAARC) and in specific sectors.
- Tariff data is key if you want a **policy-based measure of openness** (not just trade/GDP).

**How to access:**

- Web interface: https://wits.worldbank.org  
  - “Quick Search” for India; then choose “Tariffs” or “Trade Flows”.
- You can download CSV/Excel tables for further analysis.

---

## 3. IMF – Direction of Trade Statistics (DOTS) & Balance of Payments (BOP)

**What it’s good for:**

- **DOTS**: Bilateral exports and imports between India and its trading partners (annual, some quarterly).
- **BOP/IFS**: Exports/imports of goods and services, current account, capital and financial flows.

**Why it’s useful:**

- DOTS is the standard reference for bilateral trade flows.
- BOP series help you link trade openness to **external balance and macro-stability**.

**How to access:**

- IMF Data: https://data.imf.org  
  - Datasets: “Direction of Trade Statistics (DOTS)”, “Balance of Payments (BOP)”.
- Many series are free with registration; some require institutional access.

---

## 4. OECD – Services Trade Restrictiveness Index (STRI)

**What it’s good for (services trade policy openness):**

From the search:

- *Services trade restrictiveness index (STRI)* (`OECD_STRI_STRI`)  
- Subcomponents like:
  - *Restrictions on foreign entry* (`OECD_STRI_CLAS1_1`)
  - *Barriers to competition* (`OECD_STRI_CLAS1_4`)
  - *Regulatory transparency* (`OECD_STRI_CLAS1_5`)

**Why it’s useful:**

- Quantifies **regulatory barriers to trade in services** in sectors like finance, telecom, transport, etc.
- Very relevant if your project is about **economic integration in services** and regulatory openness.

**How to access:**

- OECD STRI portal: https://www.oecd.org/trade/topics/services-trade  
- Data can be downloaded in CSV/Excel; India is covered for many sectors.

---

## 5. World Economic Forum – Global Competitiveness Index (GCI) “Trade openness” pillar

From the search:

- `WEF_GCI_GCI4_C_07_02` – *GCI 4.0: Trade openness*

**What it’s good for:**

- Composite indicator capturing aspects like tariff and non-tariff barriers, trade facilitation, etc.
- Good for an **institutional/qualitative angle** on openness, not just raw trade volumes.

**Why it’s useful:**

- Provides an index that can be compared across countries and over a short time span (for GCI 4.0, around 2017–2019).

**How to access:**

- WEF Reports: “Global Competitiveness Report” (2018, 2019)  
- Data downloads (Excel) usually linked from the report’s page on the WEF website.

---

## 6. UN Comtrade – Detailed Bilateral Trade Data

**What it’s good for:**

- Very granular (product-level) bilateral trade data for India:
  - By partner country.
  - By HS code and year.

**Why it’s useful:**

- Ideal if you want to study:
  - Integration with specific partners/regions.
  - Changes in India’s export/import basket over time.
  - Trade concentration/diversification.

**How to access:**

- UN Comtrade: https://comtradeplus.un.org  
- Requires free registration for bulk downloads; there are API options as well.

---

## 7. WTO – Trade Policy Reviews, Tariff Profiles, and Regional Trade Agreements

**What it’s good for:**

- Official documentation on India’s trade policy, including:
  - Bound and applied tariffs (aggregates and by sector).
  - Non-tariff measures, trade defense instruments.
  - Participation in **regional trade agreements (RTAs)**.

**Why it’s useful:**

- Essential for the **qualitative and institutional side** of economic integration:
  - How India’s commitments and agreements have evolved.
  - Links to SAFTA, India–ASEAN FTA, etc.

**How to access:**

- WTO Data: https://data.wto.org  
- WTO Tariff Profiles and Trade Policy Reviews: https://www.wto.org (search “India Trade Policy Review”).

---

## 8. Regional Integration / Openness Indexes

Depending on your angle, you might want composite “integration” measures:

- **Asian Development Bank (ADB)**:
  - *Asia-Pacific Regional Cooperation and Integration Index (ARCII)* – includes India in regional integration metrics (trade, investment, money/finance, people).
- **KOF Globalisation Index** (ETH Zurich):
  - Provides an index of **economic globalisation**, including trade and financial openness, for India over time.

**Why they’re useful:**

- Give a **single index** that summarizes multiple dimensions of openness/integration.
- Good for regression work or for comparing India to peers.

**Access:**

- ADB ARCII: via ADB’s data library (search “ARCII ADB data”).  
- KOF Globalisation Index: https://kof.ethz.ch (data download section).

## 9. India-Specific Official Sources

To complement international databases:

1. **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**  
   - Handbook of Statistics on the Indian Economy.  
   - Data on exports, imports, invisibles, balance of payments, exchange rates, etc.  
   - https://www.rbi.org.in → “Database on Indian Economy”.

2. **Ministry of Commerce & Industry – Department of Commerce**  
   - Detailed export/import data, sometimes by partner and product.  
   - Trade policy documents and notifications.  
   - https://commerce.gov.in and the “Export Import Data Bank” (https://tradestat.commerce.gov.in).

3. **NITI Aayog and Economic Surveys (Ministry of Finance)**  
   - Analytical chapters on trade policy, FTAs, and integration trends.  
   - Economic Survey volumes are especially useful for context and interpretation.
